# Brinewatch

A one-dock salt village on a grey reach. The **Salt Lamp**, the ferry slip, the **brine-well** behind the **net racks**, the **dory house**, and a clerkless **customs post** are the whole map. South along wet shingle: **Grey Spit**, a smaller hamlet that sells smoked eel and rumors. North is empty coast.

People measure time in tides and tabs, not hours. Ceramic bits are money. Iron is worth a stare. Rope is spoken of the way inland folk speak of weather.

## Tide Choir

The Tide Choir only sings on fog-neap nights. Locals treat it as weather, not religion. Holt Gann will tell you the pitch, not the meaning. Visitors who call it a church get corrected once, then ignored.

## Factions and work

- **Dock Hands** — day labor; Ren's people. Paid by the trip, the barrel, or the ugly job.
- **Salt Lamp regulars** — Mira's tab book is the closest thing to a census. She writes a private cipher in the margin; she will not explain it.
- **Ferry traffic** — Kell Reed's slip. Grey Spit and back, sometimes a night hop if the water is a liar and the coin is not.
- **The empty post** — no clerk, no stamps, no legal cargo. Smugglers use the word "post" as a joke. The **Cinder Skiff** is the boat people stop naming when a stranger leans in.

## Ecology

Brine, bladderwrack, gulls, and a well that tastes of metal. Fish run thicker after a fog-neap. The well water is for rinse and rope, not drinking. Drinking water comes in casks from Grey Spit when Kell feels like a supply hop.

## Ongoing

- Something bright is sometimes visible on a ledge down the brine-well.
- A drowned bell is a rumor, not a schedule.
- Customs has been empty two winters. No one has taken the desk.
