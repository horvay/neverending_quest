# World-Building — Khaldur and the Water War

Dark Sun. Athas beneath a dark sun and beside a lifeless Sea of Silt. This Campaign centers on **Khaldur**, an original Tyrian vassal city in the eastern Ringing Mountain foothills. It governs local affairs but owes Kalak tribute, troops, and obedience while he lives.

## Governing truths

- **Malkor Tain**, human defiler, former general, and conqueror of **Keth-Tal**, rules Khaldur as Kalak's vassal. He is not a sorcerer-king and cannot grant templar magic.
- The **Deep Aquifer** is stable. Malkor creates legal thirst through pumps, guarded cisterns, ration tallies, destroyed measurements, and selective closures.
- **City Templars** receive magic from Kalak. When Kalak dies, they all lose the ability to renew granted spells at once. Malkor recognizes the cause and conceals it while consolidating.
- Every arm of Khaldur authority ultimately sustains slavery, tribute, spectacle, and controlled water. Officials differ in appetite and weakness, not in allegiance to public good.
- Beneath the Arena, Rajaat's Green Age temple stands over a living **Blue Age Ruin**. Their materials, purposes, and ages are distinct.
- The **Water Artifact** is genuine late-Blue-Age Rhulisti work altered by Rajaat's autonomous contingency. It can challenge a monopoly; it cannot solve Khaldur's politics by itself.

## Khaldur

Sandstone walls climb tight steps against the foothills. Quarry dust coats the tongue; pump chains knock beneath guard whistles and Arena crowds. About 18,000 residents live here, plus caravans, slaves, and seasonal hunters.

- **Water:** physically plentiful, legally scarce.
- **Food:** hunted meat, insects, roots, caravan imports, and Arena rations tied to spectacle.
- **Materials:** bone, hardwood, stone, hide, and obsidian are ordinary. Metal is conspicuous wealth. Ceramic bits are money.
- **Law:** Malkor's decrees enforced through Kalak-empowered templars, soldiers, magistrates, and Arena staff.
- **Trade:** independent Merchant Houses import food and survival goods, often with elven partners, and carry tribute toward Tyr.

### Districts

- **Citadel Rise:** Governor's Citadel, Templar Hall, tribute yards, and Royal Cistern.
- **Arena Ward:** Arena, prison works, beast pens, gambling stalls, butcheries, and labor barracks.
- **Potters' Ward:** clay works and public pumps; City Templars once closed its water for twelve days after a tax protest, killing eleven and driving dozens into debt bondage.
- **Cutters' Ward:** quarries, masons, and slave compounds.
- **Low Market:** merchants, fences, caravan yards, and tally exchange.
- **Western Waterworks:** maintenance shafts, Under Toe neighborhoods, and approaches to the aquifer.
- **Broken Chain Square:** bare plinth where Malkor forced the mul sculptor Dhorren to destroy his chain-breaking statue before execution. Guards scrape away new charcoal marks every morning.

## Political factions

### City Templars

Formal coercive institution. Controls ration tallies, pump schedules, prison labor, permits, archives, and legal force. Needs Malkor's soldiers and Kalak's continued life.

- **High Templar Veska:** institution first; keeps proof of Malkor's false scarcity and tribute fraud for survival bargaining.
- **Jorad Pell:** Arena magistrate; converts arrest, hunger, and rigged spectacle into wealth.
- **Miraq:** cistern adjudicator whose public records show a falling aquifer while private gauges show stability.
- **Tassu Venn:** junior templar who values accurate records as leverage, not civic virtue.

Internal fracture: Veska protects continuity, Jorad protects Arena profit, Miraq sells allotments, and Tassu weighs which betrayal best advances him after Kalak's power fails.

### Under Toe

Halfling-supremacist gang descended chiefly from enslaved Keth-Tal survivors. Reclaimed Khaldur's "under toe" slur. Controls crawlspaces, stolen pump keys, kin networks, and defended neighborhoods. Wants Malkor dead, templars removed, and the Deep Aquifer under halfling control—not universal liberation.

Under Toe accepts eating non-halfling sentients and treats halfling flesh as taboo. This is culture and choice, not biology. Its central tension is whether halfling safety requires supremacy, separation, or negotiated control.

- **Rava:** political leader and Keth-Tal survivor.
- **Nok:** savage Arena enforcer.
- **Lokee:** healer who argues that supremacy enforced through thirst repeats Malkor's crime.
- **Ukos:** waterworks scout and ambush organizer.

### Stone Carvers

Semi-organized resistance named for Dhorren. Controls tools, construction knowledge, and hidden stores. It can sabotage Malkor but lacks hope, coordination, or a shared plan for surviving victory.

- **Korrik:** completely cynical leader of the Arena cell.
- **Sella Marr:** courier who still expects a coordinated revolt.
- **Brakk:** prefers destructive attacks even when coerced workers bear the cost.
- **Yara Pell:** wants sabotage redirected before collective punishment reaches her family and shelter.

### Masks

Profit-seeking thieves' guild: burglary, fencing, smuggling, extortion, stolen records, and bribed routes. It wants predictable corruption and open markets, not liberation.

- **Veyra:** guildmaster; prefers profitable officials to dead ones and holds copied aquifer reports.
- **Renn:** nihilistic Arena cell leader.
- **Toma Ash:** fence for seals and ration tallies.
- **Kesh:** favors direct gang rule and violent extortion.
- **Laro:** protects an elven smuggling route.

### Merchant Houses

A politically and morally mixed commercial sector, not a united faction. Controls imported food, beasts, credit, and route intelligence; needs water allotments, road security, and predictable law.

- **Tirren Vale:** caravan organizer who refuses slave cargo and believes Pauk died in custody.
- **Hadrik Sol:** tribute master and conservative stabilizer.
- **Sada Marr:** treats enslaved people as high-risk cargo.
- **Elessa:** prioritizes her elven trade partners over Khaldur's political claims.

## The Arena

The public Arena is maintained fitted stone with imported sand berms, working gates, beast pens, an imperial box, and an announcer structure. **Malkor calls the bouts himself**. Jorad writes slates and tallies; if Malkor leaves, Jorad may take the horns or the horns go silent.

The prisoner works beneath it belong to different eras:

1. **Intake Court:** search, confiscation, branding or harnessing, and ownership records.
2. **Sacrifice Ward:** circular first sublevel with sixteen wedge-shaped ancient metal cages around a central guard table. Rajaat killed or drained captives here to provide the initial charge for the Long Seal. Modern guards mistake the channels under the cages for drainage.
3. **Arena Barracks:** compact nine-area working guardhouse between the ward and Old Work Gate. A normal shift contains fifteen guards, including Captain Hadran Cross, and three enslaved workers.
4. **Old Work Gate:** physically locked below the Southern Guard Post. Twelve slaves were sent down months ago; three survive in the Abandoned Foundation Hall.
5. **Long Seal:** horizontal timed barrier in the next chamber's floor. Its fixed span ended several weeks before the Campaign; open stairs descend through its center. No current sacrifice or bloodline sustains it.

### Arena pressures

- **Arena Intake:** Sava Tel and Mera Vonn search and record; Boros Henn brands or harnesses; Kel Sorn issues one basic nonmetal weapon before an anakore exhibition.
- **Blood and Rations:** Jorad diverts prisoner food to a gambling feast. Emergency stores could feed every cell for days. Jankx make scattered food a rescue-and-movement problem; Orren Kadd values an agony beetle more than its living host.
- **Cell Wars:** Jorad circulates forged betrayal evidence and schedules friendly prisoners against each other. Factions offer tools, food, routes, or numbers at moral and political prices.
- **Grand Melee:** Malkor promises that one side leaves fed and free while Jorad rigs waves and Cross prepares reinforcements. Food and water move behind the reserve gate. A passing Tyrian military column camps around the Arena for the spectacle; it was never sent to hunt the prisoners.

Public gates, the undercroft loading yard, the imperial box, the announcer structure, and the Old Work Gate are all real escape approaches. The transit camp makes surface exits dangerous and visible, never impossible. A lost grand melee can move survivors to the west holding run under a disposal order, creating another playable route instead of off-screen death.

## Rajaat's Temple and Blue Age Ruin

### Foundation Hall and Long Seal

One Abandoned Foundation Hall lies beyond the Old Work Gate. Three survivors from the earlier expedition hold a barricade there. The next chamber contains the Long Seal laid flat across the floor with broad open stairs through its dead center.

### Rajaat's Temple

Black Green Age masonry, dead organic grafts, and research chambers built over something older. Historical layers include a Threshold Gallery, Hall of Measures, graft garden, Seal Engine, and passages exposing the material mismatch with the Blue Age Ruin. Rajaat's captive experiments and later grafts are evidence, not a voice issuing live commands.

### Blue Age Ruin

Smooth living walls, dry mineral channels, color deepening around warm bodies, and child-sized Rhulisti controls. A Sea Memory Gallery shows currents, rainfall, and the Brown Tide without producing water. A Maintenance Reef holds dormant life-shaped tools. Original systems try to quarantine Rajaat's graft; the graft tries to use nearby halflings as authorization.

### Rhulisti Vault and Water Artifact

Any willing halfling can wake and operate the artifact. One operator bonds at a time. The palm-sized cartilage-like device settles beneath the skin at the skull base, almost invisible except for green eyes and faint moving **Living Marks**.

- Bonding takes about 1 hour and produces 30 gallons of **Resistant Water** once per day.
- Resistant Water stays pure for several days and resists contamination to an intentionally unknown degree. It is not a universal antidote.
- Output doubles with later maturation. Transfer to another halfling resets output to 30 gallons; bodily changes already acquired remain.
- **Rhulisti Reversion** is permanent, pleasant, and vitalizing: shorter, lighter, wirier, smoother-skinned, less hairy, short-haired, large-eyed, green-eyed.
- **Memory Visions** begin with truthful Blue Age wonder, then omit disagreement and recast later peoples as usurpers. They persuade toward restoration and extermination without mind control.
- The dark graft is Rajaat's later work. His contingency aims to create a halfling restoration leader while Rajaat remains imprisoned.

Lifting the artifact from its cradle irreversibly opens the **Maintenance Rise** and starts an exact 1-hour grace period. From minute 0 through 59 the ruin remains completely stable: no cave-ins, falling ribs, or collapse-driven closures. Six pale bands count down ten-minute intervals. Collapse begins after the hour; the Maintenance Rise climbs to a concealed opening in the western quarry outside Khaldur's wall.

## Surface situations

- **Water Under Khaldur:** every distribution model creates an access point and a gatekeeper. Public outlet, faction control, merchant contract, mobile reserve, and secrecy each produce different dependencies.
- **Broken Chain:** Stone Carvers escalate from symbols to structural strikes after sabotage loses a coherent target. Their actions can destroy pumps, bridges, and coerced workers unless redirected.
- **Under Toe Rising:** Rava advances from kin defense to pump seizures, protected districts, and a halfling ward-state. Safety gained for one population may be enforced through thirst for another.
- **False Scarcity:** physical measurements, official records, and witnesses can prove the aquifer is stable. Publication still requires a trusted carrier, protection from retaliation, and an answer to forged counterclaims.
- **Deep Aquifer:** Malkor controls pumps but not the whole cavern. Under Toe knows narrow approaches. A territorial cistern fiend reacts violently to large-scale pumping changes and can be driven off, redirected, bargained around through ecology, or killed.
- **Templar Silence:** Kalak's death causes simultaneous magical failure. News from Tyr, repeated spell tests, and the absence of renewed magic reveal the cause. Malkor, Veska, and Tassu each decide what to conceal or sell.
- **Governor Without a Patron:** Malkor's endgame can be answered by institutional coup, public revolt, targeted infiltration, economic isolation, Under Toe offensive, Stone Carver strike, negotiated settlement, or continued rule under enforceable limits.

## Portable revelations

Move each fact to at least two people or places the player engages.

1. Arena food is deliberately diverted: kitchen loads, Jorad's ledger, Pauk's caravan expertise.
2. Grand-melee reinforcements are assigned in advance: guard orders, Tala's observation, stolen red tally.
3. The Old Work Gate reaches older construction and the deeper timed seal has expired: duty slate, guard rumor, dead magical vibration, cage channels, Masks plans.
4. Rajaat used captives for the Long Seal's initial charge: Sacrifice Ward grooves, Seal Engine records, survivor sketch.
5. Rajaat built over a Blue Age ruin: material mismatch, reliefs, Rhulisti-sized controls.
6. The artifact is genuine Rhulisti work and the dark graft is later: original surfaces, graft scars, repeated temple marks, mismatched terminology.
7. Any halfling can operate it: original controls, vault responses to different halflings.
8. The aquifer is stable: gauge rods, private ledgers, Merchant House pump loads.
9. Rava omits human aid during Keth-Tal's fall: sealed clan record, auxiliary cairn, survivor testimony.
10. Veska keeps evidence against Malkor: Tassu, Masks copies, sealed archive.
11. Kalak's death caused universal templar failure: simultaneous tests, Tyr news, no renewed spells.
12. Memory Visions curate truth toward extermination: missing disagreement, repeated viewpoint, Rajaat's later language.

## Ecology and encounter roles

- **Anakore:** burrows through imported sand berms, not fitted Arena stone; gates and plinths create counterplay.
- **Jankx:** food and movement pressure around prone prisoners and scattered rations.
- **Agony beetles:** extraction, quarantine, and a living host problem; failure need not kill the host.
- **Hej-kin:** temple salvage negotiation or vertical skirmish.
- **Graft-garden organisms:** heat-seeking restraint hazard; cooling, stealth, decoys, cutting, purity signals, or fire can answer it.
- **Cilops:** scent-driven city pursuit and patrol pressure.
- **Cistern fiend:** territorial aquifer boss whose behavior has nonlethal ecological answers.

## Themes

1. Culture shapes; ancestry does not decide.
2. Liberation does not supply government.
3. Scarcity is political.
4. Survival leaves marks on bodies, debts, and institutions.
5. Useful power creates a distribution problem before it creates freedom.

## Content

Slavery, starvation, branding, humiliation, cannibalism, racial supremacism, body transformation, torture, and institutional violence. Keep established lines and veils active. Preserve player agency over PC beliefs, bonding, and permanent injury.
